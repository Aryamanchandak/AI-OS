# Executive Summary

The discussion focused on Sun Pharma’s Ilumya (tildrakizumab) slowdown in the U.S. psoriasis market. The market remains structurally attractive and underpenetrated, but Ilumya appears disadvantaged versus higher-efficacy products such as Skyrizi and Tremfya, particularly in commercial formularies. Expert commentary suggests Ilumya is primarily used in Medicare buy-and-bill settings and for patients requiring in-office administration, rather than as a preferred first-line biologic.

The likely causes of the slowdown include saturation of Ilumya’s Medicare-focused niche, loss of volume share to stronger brands, and limited formulary access. The issue is more likely volume-related than driven by recent rebate negotiations. A near-term recovery is uncertain unless Sun can improve formulary positioning, expand into other channels or geographies, or strengthen its clinical differentiation.

# Key Discussion Points

- **U.S. psoriasis market dynamics**
  - Psoriasis accounts for roughly two-thirds of Pfizer’s relevant revenue discussed.
  - The market is efficacy- and clinical-data-driven, with strong competition from entrenched products.
  - Biologic penetration remains relatively low, meaning the overall market is still expanding.

- **Competitive positioning**
  - Skyrizi is viewed as the gold-standard biologic, supported by stronger PASI 90/100 efficacy data, extensive clinical trials, and favorable formulary positioning.
  - Skyrizi’s commercial strength is reinforced by AbbVie’s ability to offer substantial rebates through its broader product portfolio.
  - Tremfya and other leading brands are also competing aggressively for patients, including those transitioning from Stelara.
  - Ilumya does not appear to have a clear efficacy advantage and is instead positioned around long-term safety, durability, and convenience in specific settings.

- **Ilumya’s clinical and commercial niche**
  - Ilumya’s efficacy appears meaningfully below Skyrizi on PASI 90 and PASI 100 measures.
  - Its main use cases may include:
    - Medicare buy-and-bill reimbursement.
    - In-office administration for patients considered unreliable with self-administered treatment.
    - Situations where physicians value scheduled administration and compliance.
  - IL-23 therapies are generally viewed as having clean safety profiles, reducing safety differentiation as a prescribing factor.

- **Formulary and payer considerations**
  - Formulary negotiations are generally revisited toward year-end, but the observed decline in Ilumya’s volume suggests the problem is likely not solely related to recent rebate negotiations.
  - The decline may reflect actual volume-share loss to Skyrizi, Tremfya, Stelara, or other competing products.
  - The key payer relationships to monitor include UnitedHealthcare, CVS/Aetna, and Humana, which collectively represent a large portion of the market.
  - Payer relationships are difficult to assess independently because managed-care companies rarely comment on individual brands.

- **Market saturation and patient persistence**
  - Ilumya may have reached saturation within its Medicare buy-and-bill niche.
  - If the market is saturated, future revenue could be driven mainly by price reductions and higher rebates rather than volume growth.
  - A key risk is that existing patients discontinue Ilumya or switch therapies faster than Sun can replace them with new patients.

- **Impact of oral therapies**
  - Oral therapies such as Icotro may delay the use of biologics by adding another treatment step before patients move to products such as Skyrizi or Tremfya.
  - However, current clinical data do not indicate that Icotro is clearly superior to Skyrizi, so it is not viewed as an immediate major threat to best-in-class biologics.
  - Oral therapies could be more problematic for products with modest efficacy, such as Ilumya.

- **Ex-U.S. opportunity**
  - Sun reportedly continues to perform better outside the U.S.
  - Adoption of injectable therapies may differ internationally because physician reimbursement incentives vary between intravenous and subcutaneous administration.

# Important Decisions

- The current evidence points more toward **volume-share loss and/or saturation** than a recent rebate-related issue.
- Ilumya should not be considered a best-in-class efficacy product relative to Skyrizi.
- Safety is unlikely to provide a meaningful competitive advantage because the broader IL-23 class is generally regarded as safe.
- Ilumya’s commercial relevance appears concentrated in a narrower Medicare buy-and-bill and in-office administration niche.
- Skyrizi’s strong efficacy, clinical evidence, and payer economics make it difficult for Ilumya to gain preferred formulary status.
- The appropriate analytical focus is not only whether growth can resume, but whether Ilumya could experience an absolute revenue decline.
