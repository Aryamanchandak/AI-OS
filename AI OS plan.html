Review (3 Nov)

A broader look than the code: the capstone handoff, the repo as it is, and the two old pages side by side. Three cuts are already made on this page: the tiers, the principles (down to six) and the after-submission detail. The originals are in the backup. Everything else here is a proposal.

Missing ideas

A purpose line. The old page had twelve principles about how to build and none about why. The handoff has one, and it’s the best idea in the project: everyday AI use runs up a “cognitive debt”, and this system exists to hand judgment back to you through commit-then-check, the counter-case and calibration. Put that sentence, plus the capstone’s one-sentence deliverable, at the top of stages.md. It’s the test for saying no to new features.

A measure of whether it works, not just whether it runs. Every “done when” checks that something is used. Nothing checks that you got better. The data is already planned:

  • grade accuracy per topic over time;
  • your Brier score over time;
  • how often the steelman changed your mind.

One weekly number from the store would answer the pitch’s “how I’ll know it works”.

The fading scaffold. The handoff names it as the next layer: the system does less on a kind of question once your calibration on it is good. It’s the natural end state of the purpose above, and it appears nowhere in the plan. It belongs at the top of the after-submission list.

Links between the asks. Right now the five asks are five tools. What makes them one system is the links between them:

  • a contradicted claim becomes a card;
  • a mistake pattern becomes a decision assumption;
  • “what now?” checks open decisions.

Claim check already reads cards. Name one more link per stage, or say plainly that links come after submission.

Running costs and privacy, written once. The privacy risks are already noted in three places:

  • Telegram chats aren’t end-to-end encrypted;
  • Langfuse Cloud traces leave your machine;
  • Instinct’s terms grant a broad licence over what you send it.

None of them is written up together. Before anything sits at a public URL, it needs one short paragraph covering:

  • a monthly model-spend cap, since CI evals and the assistant loop both cost per run;
  • where the calendar OAuth refresh token lives (the handoff says real secure storage, not just .env);
  • what personal data the public demo can see.

Levels, used for “done”. Each node’s current and minimum level is in the backup of the old page:

  • 0: not started.
  • 1: runs once, by hand.
  • 2: usable daily, meaning its eval file passes and every run is traced.
  • 3: reliable, meaning evals gate every change in CI and it survives restarts.
  • 4: refined, meaning real failures become eval cases before fixes.
