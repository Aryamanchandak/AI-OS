- Author: [[Jared Bernstein]]
- Full Title: I Now Believe Our National Debt Is a Problem
- Category: #articles
- Summary: The U.S. national debt has grown to equal the size of the economy, raising risks of higher interest costs and a debt spiral. Tax cuts that do not pay for themselves have driven much of this debt increase, making the fiscal outlook worrisome. To fix this, the country needs fairer taxes on wealth and smarter spending, but political challenges make solutions difficult.

## Highlights
- What changed? The national debt held by the public, about $31 trillion, is now the size of the U.S. economy, up from 39 percent of the economy in 2008 and 79 percent in 2019. For [most of the country’s history](https://www.cbpp.org/research/difference-between-economic-growth-rates-and-treasury-interest-rates-significantly-affects), the fact that the economy’s growth rate surpassed the interest rate on the debt enabled us to keep paying our bills. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0t9fp3c2qx5ge093ejd3f))
- We concluded that the combination of higher deficits and climbing interest rates raises the risk that borrowing will become more expensive and will push government debt levels to climb relentlessly. This is a debt spiral. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0vr3a2913vqsv8xk9216q))
- President Trump’s war in Iran, which is putting upward pressure on inflation, has led lenders to insist on extra compensation—that is, higher interest rates—to offset inflation’s erosion of the value of future payments ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0wyh2wt5at2e602y8mkhd))
- debt investors also know that the government will have to issue trillions of dollars in debt in the coming years. And with all of that debt flooding the market, the government will have to offer higher rates to keep its creditors in the game. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0xf7vyv2vyrzjx1mja945))
- Creditors are [already insisting](https://econjared.substack.com/p/updating-my-prior-that-outside-of) on higher “term premiums,” meaning higher compensation in the form of higher interest rates when they buy our debt. ⁠ ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0z3me7b8jghhcdt8z4j3r))
- Higher rates mean higher debt service, and the net interest is already the fastest growing part of the budget, which means we’re on the edge of a troubling feedback loop. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0yzhse90qrzaqx5rx1rws))
- President Ronald Reagan insisted that his tax cuts, which mostly benefited the rich, would generate enough extra growth to both offset their costs and trickle down to the middle class. The cuts simply grew the deficit instead. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk0zw8194se33a63bag9j0x))
- When Treasury Secretary Paul O’Neill warned Vice President Dick Cheney about the deficits the tax cuts would generate, Cheney reportedly responded, “Reagan proved deficits don’t matter.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk115amkqdwkr2435zae10z))
- I have been dovish on the budget in part because I understand that public debt, like private debt, is not all bad. Just as it is sensible for a family to borrow to invest in their kids’ college education, so too might a government sensibly borrow to invest in productive infrastructure. Both investments should yield returns that help offset the debt they incur. Borrowing and investing is economically sustainable when doing so boosts growth relative to the cost of borrowing. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk122z1p35vtg4rn09yc13j))
- Advocates of tax cuts—from Reagan’s cuts in the ’80s to Trump’s in the 2010s and ’20s—have long argued that they generate more than enough growth to pay for themselves ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk12r645y5k7nrb2qch1wmx))
- As Bobby Kogan, the senior director of federal budget policy at the Center for American Progress, and I have shown in recent [research](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/president-trumps-big-beautiful-bill-raises-the-fiscal-gap-to-2-4-percent/), tax cuts are precisely to blame for driving up the country’s debt ratio ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk13ty5nwbz7wjc2ddyhpa9))
- We’ve certainly seen consequences in other countries, most notably in the United Kingdom, where international creditors responded to a debt-inducing budget in 2022 by dumping U.K. bonds, which led to soaring interest rates and tanking currency values ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk14hn93n5aaet51eqcsewd))
- But the dollar is different. As the globe’s main reserve currency, the dollar is something that other countries need for various transactions, which they manage by holding U.S. bonds. This ensures the market for our public debt is orders of magnitude larger than any other country’s. America’s debt auctions sell whatever is issued, regardless of administration or budget. This means that the United States has a lot more leeway to deficit-spend than other countries. But this leeway is not infinite. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk159bh7jzt2kcxnw6xw4q4))
- Now consider this: Any politician who runs on reducing the deficit—who insists that Americans must now accept some combination of spending cuts and higher tax payments—will be at a sharp disadvantage against an opponent who claims that no tax hikes or spending cuts are needed, that the problems can be solved by simply cutting waste, fraud, and abuse, and by growing our way out of this mess. Who can resist a plan for eating ice cream all day without ever gaining a pound? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01ksk160jk4dnhd1jsjchz5d6b))
