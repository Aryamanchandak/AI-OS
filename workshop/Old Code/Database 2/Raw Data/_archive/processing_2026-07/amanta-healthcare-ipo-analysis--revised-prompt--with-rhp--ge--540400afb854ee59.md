Amanta Healthcare Limited: Initial Public Offering (IPO) Analysis

Recommendation: AVOID

Amanta Healthcare Limited is an established pharmaceutical company specializing in sterile liquid parenteral products (LVPs and SVPs) with integrated manufacturing and a diverse product portfolio. The IPO is a fresh issue of 10,000,000 equity shares, with proceeds intended for capital expenditure to expand manufacturing capacity.

While the company operates in a growing sector and possesses significant manufacturing capabilities, our analysis reveals several material red flags that present a high-risk profile for institutional investors. These include a high debt-to-equity ratio, a history of debt settlement following a default, significant pledging of promoter shares, and outstanding criminal and tax litigation against the company and its Managing Director. Although the company returned to profitability in FY24 and FY25 after a loss in FY23, its financial stability is overshadowed by governance and contingent liability concerns. The combination of these risk factors outweighs the potential operational strengths, leading to our "AVOID" recommendation.

Amanta Healthcare operates in a structurally sound industry with favorable long-term demand drivers. The company's plan to expand its manufacturing capacity for higher-margin SteriPort and SVP products through the IPO is a logical step to capture future growth. Operationally, it demonstrates superior EBITDA margins compared to its listed peer.

However, the investment thesis is severely undermined by a multitude of significant red flags. The company's balance sheet is highly leveraged, and its past inability to service debt, leading to a major settlement, cannot be overlooked. The pledging of a substantial portion of the promoter's stake adds another layer of financial risk. Most critically, the unresolved criminal and tax litigations represent material contingent liabilities and a serious governance concern.

For an institutional investor, the combination of a weak balance sheet, past financial distress, and significant governance/legal overhangs makes this a high-risk proposition. The potential for operational growth does not adequately compensate for these fundamental weaknesses. Therefore, we recommend AVOID.
