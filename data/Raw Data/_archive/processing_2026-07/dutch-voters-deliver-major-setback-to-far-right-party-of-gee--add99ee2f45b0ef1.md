- The center-left party, Democrats 66, was projected to win 27 seats in the 150-seat House of Representatives, the largest share, followed closely by Geert Wilders’ far-right Party for Freedom, with a projected 25 seats. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01k8t9990by8eg34gvat0mpa31))
- In July, Mr. Wilders [withdrew his party](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/06/03/world/europe/geert-wilders-netherlands-coalition.html) from the governing coalition, saying it was stalling plans for what he promised would be the country’s “strictest migration policy ever.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01k8t98srzxqf8ss3p7ftjve98))
- By excluding the PVV, it will now fall to parties in the political center to work together to get the majority needed to form a government.
  D66 has the ability to bridge the gap between the right and the left, said Simon Otjes, an assistant professor of Dutch politics at Leiden University. The party, he said, moved slightly to the right in this campaign. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01k8t9a3jwf5t5xrvkdxfnshe5))
- Just because a center-left party seems to have won the largest share of the votes does not mean that it decisively beat populism, Professor Otjes said. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01k8t9ae5y8yxa40ed0ce0cszp))
- Mr. Wilders’s party has long been seen as focused on the issue of migration, which appealed to many voters. But more [centrist parties](https://europa.vvd.nl/standpunten/migratie/) have begun to put forward their own ideas for constraining immigrant flows, echoing a [trend across Europe](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/06/29/world/europe/europe-migration-crackdown-trump.html). ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01k8t9bcsh23etbwx1z1m8wvee))
