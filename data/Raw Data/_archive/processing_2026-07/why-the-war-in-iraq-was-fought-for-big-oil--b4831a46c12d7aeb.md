## Metadata
- Author: [[Antonia Juhasz]]
- Full Title: Why the War in Iraq Was Fought for Big Oil
- Category: #articles
- Summary: The war in Iraq was fought largely to open the country's oil sector to Western companies. After Saddam Hussein was removed, U.S. advisors helped change Iraq’s oil policies to benefit these companies. Many Iraqis and activists believe this harmed Iraq’s sovereignty and protested against foreign control of their oil.

- They also provide exceptionally long contract terms and high ownership stakes and eliminate requirements that Iraq’s oil stay in Iraq, that companies invest earnings in the local economy or hire a majority of local workers. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kmx7gcgrye554ahv2ysaqbcq))
- Iraq’s oil production has increased by more than 40% in the past five years to [3 million barrels of oil a day](http://www.worldenergyoutlook.org/iraq/) (still below the 1979 high of 3.5 million set by Iraq’s state-owned companies), but a full 80% of this is being exported out of the country while Iraqis struggle to meet basic energy consumption needs. GDP per capita has increased significantly yet remains among the lowest in the world and well below some of Iraq’s other oil-rich neighbors. Basic services such as water and electricity remain luxuries, while [25% of the population](http://www.worldenergyoutlook.org/iraq/) lives in poverty. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kmx7gv121hhss0fbtx53nvcx))

([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kmx7h29pqejdkqj82kcx3kfv))
