

AI-assisted reading technologies—exemplified by systems like NotebookLM—are reshaping knowledge acquisition. These tools ingest long-form documents and output condensed summaries, contextual explanations, conceptual maps, and personalised learning pathways. Their advantages include accelerated comprehension, accessibility for diverse learners, and the democratisation of specialised knowledge. Yet they also introduce risks: erosion of deep reading capacity, homogenisation of interpretation, cognitive dependency, and potential downstream effects on democratic culture.

This paper presents a rigorous and cohesive analysis of the promises and perils of AI-mediated reading. It argues that their adoption creates a structural tension: they enhance individual learning speeds while simultaneously undermining the very cognitive skills—attention, interpretive autonomy, and critical reasoning—that meaningful learning and democratic citizenship require.

A new framework is proposed for optimal use: AI-augmented deep learning, which integrates rapid comprehension with deliberate preservation of long-form engagement and independent synthesis.
