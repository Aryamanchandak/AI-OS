## Highlights
- In the U.S., a baby is born every 9 seconds. A person dies every 9 seconds. An international migrant enters the country every 90 seconds. This means the U.S. gains one person every 57 seconds, according to the Census Bureau's U.S. and World Population Clock ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mnxge782a78tv1r19c200))
- But demographers anticipate that the population will peak over the next 55 years and then begin a rapid decline. Researchers don’t agree on what the consequences of a falling fertility rate might mean for both society and science, and that’s because population loss may look very different from one part of the globe to the next. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mpab03m21k49npexpkddh))
- What Is Needed for Steady Population Growth? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mpfwf4dxarmk4zdwcmq9a))
- For the population to remain steady, the average woman needs to have [2.1 children](https://www.discovermagazine.com/scientists-debate-why-childbirth-is-so-brutal-43864) in her lifetime. The average replacement rate, however, has been falling in the past few decades, according to a report in the *American Economic Review*. The U.S. is currently at a replacement rate of 1.8, and the world average for wealthy countries is 1.7, although some countries like Italy, Japan, and Spain are lower. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mpm4fzgz51xfhd20prq3m))
- By 2023, the OECD average had dropped to 1.54, well below the replacement rate. The global average is also expected to drop to 2.1 by 2050. This could mean the global population, currently around 8.3 billion, could peak at 10.3 billion in 2084 and then begin to decline, according to Bloom. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mpwqyjhfb95dy2t0zgfyn))
- In the U.S., there’s a lack of desire: 57 percent of childfree people under 50 say they don’t want to be parents, according to Pew Research Center findings. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mq701e26k62nt43tsb1xx))
- In China, the one-child policy prevented families from having more than one child from 1980 to 2015. The policy was meant to prevent a population explosion, but China is now expected to experience one of the most drastic population declines. In the next 75 years, the Chinese population is expected to decline by 786 million people, which is half of their current population, according to the United Nations. This means the size of the Chinese population in 2100 will resemble that of 1950. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mqdv8xqf1d80tw92zdv91))
- In the U.S., immigration is helping to keep the population stable. But without immigration, the U.S. population would drop by 100 million by 2100, about one-third of its current size, according to research from Brookings. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mqg1kvc7j4mb61y6sqs8h))
- What Population Decline Means for the Future ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mqjx5b3jbptr6a05125cp))
- There are many ideas about how falling fertility rates will change a society. One concern is that an aging population will lack a younger [workforce](https://www.discovermagazine.com/ants-make-individuals-weaker-to-build-bigger-societies-48437) to provide care services or to pay the taxes needed to fund eldercare benefits or services, according to the International Monetary Fund. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mqxzzehk74kdqrgmx0064))
- Another concern is that science and innovation could be halted when the stream of people entering these fields decreases to a mere trickle. Fewer people means fewer ideas. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kr3mr8y05d4am5de0vy5krdr))
