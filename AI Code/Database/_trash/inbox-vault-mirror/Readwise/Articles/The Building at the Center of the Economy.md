## Highlights
- The AI boom is visible from orbit. Satellite photos of New Carlisle, Indiana, show greenish splotches of farmland transformed into unmistakable industrial parks in less than a year’s time. There are [seven](https://www.distilled.earth/p/these-data-centers-are-getting-really) rectangular data centers there, with 23 more on the way. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsfqkrx0xdb31j2mqqmx2f))
- When all the data centers in New Carlisle are built, they will demand more power than two Atlantas. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsftn2149jqw1bvgw05796))
- The amount of energy and money being poured into AI is breathtaking. Global spending on the technology is projected to hit $375 billion by the end of the year and half a trillion dollars in 2026. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsg38pv7x9s4p6er5p3xz2))
- Multiplying Ford’s current market cap 94 times over wouldn’t quite get you to Nvidia’s. Yet 20 years ago, Ford was worth nearly triple what Nvidia was. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadshk0fgwmc898xwq19b6e4))
- The number keeps going up, which has a buoying effect on markets that is, in the short term, good. But every good earnings report further entrenches Nvidia as a precariously placed, load-bearing piece of the global economy. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadshdz4ds48xz37f3rng7e2))
- America appears to be, at the moment, in a sort of benevolent hostage situation. AI-related spending now contributes more to the nation’s GDP growth than all consumer spending combined, and by another [calculation](https://x.com/jasonfurman/status/1971995367202775284), those AI expenditures accounted for 92 percent of GDP growth during the first half of 2025 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsjar8et0pjvfj29f7k1e2))
- Since the launch of ChatGPT, in late 2022, the tech industry has gone from making up 22 percent of the value in the S&P 500 to roughly one-third. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsjm4wdyevy775v4q1ezmq))
- OpenAI has announced that it intends to build at least 30 gigawatts’ worth of data centers—more power than all of New England requires on even the hottest day—and CEO Sam Altman has said he’d eventually like to build a gigawatt of AI infrastructure every week. Other major tech firms have similar ambitions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsrb7119fedfez1cmjrfeb))
- Listen to the AI crowd talk enough, and you’ll get a sense that we may be on the cusp of an infrastructure boom ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsyns2ebdgvvn4qnd956zk))
- yet, something strange is happening to the economy. Even as tech stocks have skyrocketed since 2022, the companies’ share of net profits from S&P 500 companies has hardly budged. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsywk87h2qh6vv2tvzhfcg))
- Job openings have fallen despite a roaring stock market, 22 states are in or near a recession, and despite data centers propping up the construction industry, U.S. manufacturing is in decline. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadsz45ws6ja318jwq3g0vr4))
- It’s clear that AI is both drowning out and obscuring other stories about the wobbling American economy. That’s a concern. But even worse: What if AI’s promise for American business proves to be a mirage? What happens then? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadszd5pqbmbbsnrn06ey2m6))
- The yawning gap between data-center expenditures and the rest of the economy has caused whispers of *bubble* to rise to a chorus ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadszr1h2cjades7h3yb2jbn))
- *The Information*, OpenAI likely made [$4 billion](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/chatgpt-subscribers-nearly-tripled-to-15-5-million-in-2024) last year but lost $5 billion (making the idea of a $1 trillion IPO valuation that much more staggering) ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadt2ajcnxbmeh2t5hp5jw3f))
- From July through September, Microsoft’s investments in OpenAI resulted in [losses](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2026-Q1/press-release-webcast) totaling more than $3 billion. For that same time period, Meta reported rapidly growing costs due to its AI investments, spooking investors and sending its stock down 9 percent. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadt2trk38mc16x80kzhx6eh))
- Chatbots and AI chips are getting more efficient almost by the day, while the business case for deploying generative-AI tools remains shaky ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadt36t3zbtqhsgchh0s9cgz))
- recent [report](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/seizing-the-agentic-ai-advantage#/) from McKinsey found that nearly 80 percent of companies using AI discovered that the technology had no significant impact on their bottom line ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtc7xcz57n7x4na93z9ng6))
- The economic nightmare scenario is that the unprecedented spending on AI doesn’t yield a profit anytime soon, if ever, and data centers sit at the center of those fears ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtcyxcmhys3sbc1q1ey9yg))
- But at some point in the near future, data-center spending will likely outpace even these enormous cash flows, reducing Big Tech’s liquidity and worrying investors. And so, as the AI arms race continues to escalate, the companies are beginning to [raise](https://fortune.com/2025/10/24/debt-financing-ai-tech-stock-market-weaker-morgan-stanley/) outside money—in other words, take on debt. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtezbr4ph8kajh9ea5r1dm))
- Tech firms don’t want to formally take on debt—that is, directly ask investors for loans—because debt looks bad on their balance sheets and could reduce shareholder returns. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtfksw0f3p7vc8a7jtkrmk))
- These private-equity firms put up or raise the money to build a data center, which a tech company will repay through rent. Data-center leases from, say, Meta can then be repackaged into a financial instrument that people can buy and sell—a bond, in essence. Meta recently did just this: Blue Owl Capital raised money for a massive Meta data center in Louisiana by, in essence, issuing bonds backed by Meta’s rent ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtgpzz4q52e8njamrahyes))
- multiple data-center leases can be combined into a security and sorted into what are called “tranches” based on their risk of default. Data centers represent an [$800 billion market](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-10-02/ai-data-centers-give-private-credit-its-mojo-back) for private-equity firms through 2028 alone. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadth6vwdkakv743npp6q4j0))
- The complexity, investment structure, and repackaging make exactly what is going on hard to parse. And if the dynamics also sound familiar, it’s because not two decades ago, the Great Recession was precipitated by banks packaging risky mortgages into tranches of securities that were falsely marketed as high-quality. By 2008, the house of cards had collapsed. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtskjgm81jpg3y72myh4ds))
- Data-center build-outs aren’t the same as subprime mortgages. Still, there is plenty of precarity baked into these investments. Data centers deteriorate rapidly, unlike the more durable infrastructure of canals, railroads, or even fiber-optic cables. Many of the chips inside these buildings become obsolete within a few years, when Nvidia and its competitors release the next wave of bleeding-edge AI hardware. Meanwhile, the returns on scaling up chatbots are, at present, diminishing. The improvements made by each new AI model are becoming smaller and smaller, making the idea that Silicon Valley can spend its way to superintelligence more tenuous by the day. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadttnhdz9ze2d7nhek9yhjj))
- If tech stocks fall because of AI companies failing to deliver on their promises, the [highly leveraged](https://www.project-syndicate.org/commentary/ai-bubble-will-it-cause-a-financial-crisis-by-hilary-j-allen-2025-10) hedge funds that are invested in these companies could be forced into fire sales. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtw4zcj5f735kgdkyy0fhg))
- This could create a vicious cycle, causing the financial damage to spread to pension funds, mutual funds, insurance companies, and everyday investors. As capital flees the market, non-tech stocks will also plummet: bad news for anyone who thought to play it safe and invest in, for instance, real estate. If the damage were to knock down private-equity firms (which are invested in these data centers) themselves—which manage [trillions and trillions of dollars](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2023/10/private-equity-publicly-traded-companies/675788/) in assets and constitute what is basically a global shadow-banking system—that could produce another major crash. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtwxsk9gh1sm3c2zpdz80k))
- To wit: OpenAI has agreed to [pay](https://www.wsj.com/business/openai-oracle-sign-300-billion-computing-deal-among-biggest-in-history-ff27c8fe?gaa_at=eafs&gaa_n=AWEtsqc9n3Be0G_rwS8v-DbFJ-IoRs8cUbWqia3YrdEowRoKDdlgewUlqUJA3lES7wA%3D&gaa_ts=68f905da&gaa_sig=qWteTkD63kNf8ghdtpN5mSDaALbBUbnaFNX0mfpyiH1tnxU7I5s0e9t7ffLVGrB1PEfdpcrOEwa8h6LZSP9i7A%3D%3D) $300 billion to Oracle for new computing capacity, Oracle is paying Nvidia tens of billions of dollars for [chips](https://www.ft.com/content/a9cd130f-f6bf-4750-98cc-19d87394e657) to install in one of OpenAI’s data centers, and Nvidia has agreed to [invest](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/openai-and-nvidia-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-10gw-of-nvidia-systems) up to $100 billion in OpenAI as it deploys Nvidia chips. Attempts to illustrate these circular investments have produced a series of byzantine charts that one software engineer referred to on X as “[the technocapital hyperobject at the end of time](https://x.com/GrantSlatton/status/1975982007776837737).” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtxx1rz1szmrf91er2hmdf))
- Boom and bust can feel like two sides of the same coin: Consider also that if AI companies deliver on their massive investments, it would likely mean producing a technology so capable and revolutionary that it wipes out countless jobs and sends an unprecedented shock wave through the global economy before humans have time to adapt. (Perhaps we will be unable to adapt at all.) If they fail, there will likely be unprecedented financial turmoil as well. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadtzt8yerya92aqqs3q4eqh))
- The biggest lesson of the past two decades of Silicon Valley is that Meta, Amazon, and Google—and even the newer AI labs such as OpenAI—have remade our world and have become unfathomably rich for it, all while being mostly oblivious or uninterested in the fallout. They have chased growth and scale at all costs, and largely, they’ve won. The data-center build-out is the ultimate culmination of that chase: the pursuit of scale for scale itself. In all scenarios, the outcome seems only to be real, painful disruption for the rest of us. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kadv0731j7p02smgn2vaf8xb))
