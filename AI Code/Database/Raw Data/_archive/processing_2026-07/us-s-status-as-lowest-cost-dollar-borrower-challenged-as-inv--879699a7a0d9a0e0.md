- Summary: Investors are buying bonds from global development banks because they see US government debt as riskier under President Trump. These bonds now offer yields almost as low as US Treasuries, which used to be the safest choice. This shows growing concern about US fiscal stability and a shift toward more stable dollar assets.

## Highlights
- The US’s status as the world’s lowest-cost dollar borrower faces a challenge from foreign development banks as President Donald Trump’s erratic policymaking increases the appeal of alternative haven assets ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kq6ytx33e2d2f4489tpfw4ak))
- A months-long rally in bonds issued by the likes of the World Bank, the European Investment Bank and Germany’s state-owned KfW has driven yields down to levels close to US Treasuries, the traditional haven for investors during periods of turmoil. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kq6yvhy9zn8d94s2010thezq))
- In a sign of the surging demand for so-called sovereign, supranational and agency (SSA) dollar debt, the EIB this week set a record after attracting more than $33bn of orders for a $4bn, three-year bond.
  The debt was priced to yield 3.82 per cent, just 0.04 percentage points above comparable Treasuries. Investors typically demand yields above sovereign bonds to hold the debts of other borrowers. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kq6yvthc53exbpaynxeme2b7))
