Being an outsider helped foster my street smarts, which I would define as the ability to see myself objectively and apply that same perception to others. I developed a knack for sizing up different kinds of people quickly—not always with perfect accuracy, but faster and more accurately than most. If I could read people, I could figure out how to be useful to them and get them to help me. These reflexes would become a great asset for me in the years ahead. Coming from the projects in East New York, I had more to escape, more to prove, and more to gain than a lot of people I subsequently encountered.
